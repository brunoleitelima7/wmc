The context

470,000 wildfire incidents across the US in 2023

Here are all the wildfire incidents in the US in 2023 — burning through national forests, grasslands, coastal scrub and the edges of towns.

Each colour represents a different fire agency region, and the size of each mark relates to how many acres were burned.

Is it getting better?

Why it keeps growing

Wildfires are a growing crisis across North America

  1. 01 Climate change Seasons that start earlier, run hotter and end later than the record was built to describe.
  2. 02 Drought Moisture leaves the fuel before the season opens, so an ignition that once died now carries.
  3. 03 Decades of fire suppression A century of putting fires out left forests dense with fuel that was never allowed to burn.

The figures, compiled from multiple federal and state agencies, may undercount the full scale of the crisis — and the gap is not evenly spread.

But, it’s getting better, right?

Unfortunately, in the years that we have been analysing the data, wildfire frequency and severity have continued to climb rather than level off.

The numbers in this chart are very large, but a total of 12 million acres burned is equivalent to the entire state of Maryland going up at once.

Wildfire Data Sources & Accuracy

This website is intended to provide an accurate representation of wildfire data from federal and state fire agencies. The data is complex, and thus some errors may have been made. If you find something that is incorrect, please raise an issue on GitHub and we’ll endeavour to fix it quickly.

Wildfire data is sourced from incident reports, fire perimeter updates, and air quality monitoring systems. We combine data from USFS, CAL FIRE, BLM, and NWS to provide a unified view of active fires, containment status, and smoke forecasts. If you notice inconsistencies in fire naming, acreage, or containment percentages, please let us know so we can reconcile the data.

All the data sources are listed on the source code website.
